# CS-330
Computer graphics and visualization
# 1. How do I approach designing software?
When designing software, I focus on breaking complex ideas into simpler, manageable parts. For this project, I chose to recreate a version of my real-life workspace, which included a monitor, keyboard, mouse, and water bottle. Each item was distinct enough in shape and function to make them ideal for simplified, low-polygon modeling. I developed new design skills such as analyzing real-world objects and translating them into basic 3D shapes like planes, boxes, cylinders, and spheres. My design process involved identifying each object’s components, modeling them from primitives, applying materials, and then refining the scale and position based on visual feedback. This iterative approach allowed flexibility and supported quick revisions. Going forward, this modular strategy starting with basic structure, testing, and refining can be applied in any future software project to ensure clarity, maintainability, and scalability.

# 2. How do I approach developing programs?
My approach to development focuses on modularity, reusability, and iteration. While working on this 3D scene, I created functions such as SetTransformations, SetShaderTexture, SetShaderMaterial, and SetTextureUVScale to streamline development and avoid redundant code. These utility functions made it easy to apply consistent transformations and textures across many objects without rewriting logic. I constantly iterated—adding one object at a time, reviewing its placement and appearance in the scene, and making incremental adjustments until it looked right. This trial-and-error approach helped me catch mistakes early and improve the overall layout. Compared to my early milestones, where I was still learning the basics of OpenGL and object placement, my process became more refined and organized over time. By the final milestone, I had a solid structure in place that allowed me to build quickly and confidently.

# 3. How can computer science help me in reaching my goals?
Computer science empowers me to build functional and creative digital solutions, and this project helped me understand how technical and artistic elements come together in software development. Learning computational graphics and visualization has taught me how to work with OpenGL, manage transformations, apply lighting models like Phong shading, and map textures in 3D space. These skills are directly applicable to future academic work in graphics or game development courses and can support a career in industries that rely on visual technology—such as simulation, digital design, or game development. Working on this project also helped improve my problem-solving skills and attention to detail, which are critical in any software role. Ultimately, learning how to design and develop 3D environments gives me both the confidence and the technical foundation to pursue my goals in software development and beyond.
